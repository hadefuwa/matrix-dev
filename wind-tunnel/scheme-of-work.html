Programme Aims

By the end of this scheme of work, learners will be able to confidently operate a wind tunnel, interpret aerodynamic data, and apply core principles of fluid dynamics to real engineering problems.

AIM · 01

Understand core principles

Bernoulli's principle, continuity, lift, drag and the behaviour of laminar and turbulent flow.

AIM · 02

Operate the equipment safely

Set up, calibrate and run the Matrix wind tunnel; capture meaningful, repeatable measurements.

AIM · 03

Investigate & analyse

Design controlled experiments, analyse data, and evaluate the performance of aerofoils and bluff bodies.

AIM · 04

Communicate findings

Produce technical reports, diagrams and presentations that meet industry standards.

Lesson Sequence

Each lesson pairs a CP0704 worksheet with theory input, video stimulus, a hands-on activity at the wind tunnel, and homework to consolidate.

Introduction L01

Meet the Wind Tunnel

Orientation, safety, and a tour of the equipment using the manufacturer's user manual.

  • Identify the parts of the wind tunnel and explain their function
  • Follow safe operating procedure for set-up and shutdown
  • Recognise the variables the tunnel allows you to measure

Worksheet 1 L02

Pitot-Static Tube

Measure air speed using a pitot-static tube and apply Bernoulli's equation to a U-tube manometer and digital pressure sensor.

  • Explain how a pitot-static tube measures fluid speed
  • Apply Bernoulli's equation to calculate air speed from pressure difference
  • Compare manometer and digital pressure-sensor readings; calculate Reynolds number

Worksheet 2 L03

Flow Patterns Around a Cylinder

Investigate how angle of attack affects pressure distribution around a tapped cylinder; introduce the pressure coefficient Cp.

  • Define and calculate the pressure coefficient Cp from measured data
  • Record pressure at 5° increments from 0° to 180° and tabulate results
  • Plot a Cp-vs-angle graph and produce a pressure-distribution diagram

Worksheet 3 L04

Flow Patterns Around an Aerofoil

Map the pressure distribution along a NACA0015 aerofoil using 23 surface tappings, and see how angle of attack changes it.

  • Use the 23-tapping NACA0015 aerofoil to measure static pressure along the chord
  • Calculate Cp at each tapping and plot Cp-vs-fractional-chord-distance graphs
  • Compare top- and bottom-surface profiles at 0°, 5° and 12° angle of attack

Worksheet 4 L05

Lift & Drag on an Aerofoil

Use the two-force module to measure lift and drag across angles of attack, then compare three aerofoil profiles.

  • Plot lift- and drag-vs-angle curves at fan speeds 20%, 50% and 80%
  • Compare NACA0015, S1223 and NACA4415 aerofoils and evaluate their performance

Worksheet 5 L06

Drag Coefficient

Measure drag on a range of standard shapes and calculate their drag coefficient Cd.

  • Calculate Cd from measured drag force, air speed and reference area
  • Compare Cd for cone, smooth sphere, half-sphere, rough sphere, flat surface and teardrop
  • Relate experimental Cd values to real-world design (car bodies, dimpled golf balls)

Streamlined?

Use the smoke generator to visualise streamlines and capture flow patterns around test objects on slow-motion video.
