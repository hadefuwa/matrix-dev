A 12-week, 60 Guided Learning Hour programme that takes BTEC Level 3 National Engineering learners from fluid fundamentals through to fly-by-wire, with the Matrix Wind Tunnel 125 (CP0704) as the practical spine of the course.

Learning Aims

The unit is built around three Learning Aims that progress from underlying fluid behaviour to the mechanics of full aircraft flight.

AIM A

Properties of fluids & fluid flow

Explore air density, viscosity, pressure gradients, the continuity equation and Bernoulli's theorem. Underpins everything that follows.

AIM B

Lift & drag on aerodynamic shapes

Investigate how airflow generates lift and drag on aerofoils and bluff bodies, including coefficients, stall, boundary layers and scaling.

AIM C

Stability, control & the mechanics of flight

Examine aircraft stability, primary and secondary control surfaces, manoeuvre loads and modern fly-by-wire envelope protection.

12-Week Delivery Schedule

Each row is one teaching week (~5 GLH). The "Practical / Lab" column links directly to the corresponding wind tunnel worksheet slideshow and Matrix tutorial videos where they apply.

01 Fluid Properties & Atmospheric Physics Air density, viscosity, pressure gradients. The International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model. Units, fluid statics. Calculating pressure changes with altitude. ISA atmosphere modelling spreadsheet exercise. Density measurement of air at lab conditions. Diagnostic Quiz
02 Fluid Dynamics & Continuity Incompressible flow behaviour. Continuity equation A₁V₁ = A₂V₂. Mass flow rate conservation. Streamlines & control volumes. Venturi tube tracking and flow velocity calculations. Equipment familiarisation: ▶ Assembly ▶ Set up Formal Formative Problem Set
03 Bernoulli's Theorem Energy conservation in fluid flow. Static, dynamic and total pressure. P + ½ρV² = constant. Limits and assumptions of Bernoulli. WS1 · Pitot-static tube ▶ Manual & electronic readings Lab Report Draft
04 Airfoil Geometry & Boundary Layers Chord line, camber, thickness, angle of attack α. Laminar vs turbulent boundary layer transition. Separation. WS6 · Smoke-tunnel visualisation ▶ Smoke visualisation ▶ Attaching experiments Assignment 1 Issued
(Fluid Fundamentals)
05 Lift Generation Mechanics Pressure distribution across upper / lower aerofoil surfaces. Circulation. Lift equation L = ½ρV²·S·CL. Stall. WS3 · Aerofoil pressure mapping WS4 · Lift & drag ▶ Wind tunnel software Airfoil Analysis
Peer Review
06 Drag Components & Minimisation Induced drag, parasite drag (skin friction, form, interference). Total drag curve. Lift-to-drag ratio. WS5 · Drag coefficient on standard shapes Drag curves across velocity profile Assignment 1 Submission
07 Wind Tunnel Testing & Scaling Experimental aerodynamic methods. Reynolds number Re and Mach number M scaling laws. Tunnel blockage and corrections. WS2 · Cylinder flow patterns ▶ Available experiments Wind Tunnel Data
Log Check
08 Aircraft Stability Mechanics Longitudinal, lateral and directional stability. Static vs dynamic stability modes (phugoid, dutch roll, spiral). Adjusting centre of gravity (CoG) on model gliders to map stable glide paths. Optional: re-run WS6 with stable / unstable shapes for streamline contrast. Assignment 2 Issued
(Lift, Drag & Testing)
09 Primary Flight Control Surfaces Aerodynamic action of ailerons, elevators and rudders. Pitch, roll and yaw axes. Hinge moments and control feel. Flight simulator session OR physical linkage rig inspection for control tracking and movement ratios. Control Surface
Mapping Exercise
10 Secondary Controls & High-Lift Devices Flaps, slats, slots and spoilers. Effect on the lift / drag polar. Take-off and landing configuration management. WS7 · Independent investigation (flap study) Assignment 2 Submission
11 Manoeuvre Aerodynamics & Loads Forces in steady turns, climbs and descents. Load factors (g-loading) and structural limits. Constructing V-n (velocity-load factor) manoeuvre diagrams for a chosen aircraft. Lift / load calculations at varied bank angles. Assignment 3 Issued
(Stability & Performance)
12 Modern Actuation & Flight Envelopes Fly-by-wire (FBW), artificial stability augmentation systems (SAS) and flight envelope protection. Sidestick vs yoke. Analysing block diagrams of active control loops and automated trim systems. Case study on Airbus / Boeing envelope philosophies. Assignment 3 Submission
Final Review

Assessment Structure

Three summative assignments aligned to the three Learning Aims, scaffolded by weekly formative milestones and a final review.

Assignment Learning Aim Issued / Due Deliverable
A1 Fluid Fundamentals Issued Wk 4 · Due Wk 6 Lab report on pitot-static + smoke visualisation results, with Bernoulli analysis and boundary-layer commentary.
A2 Lift, Drag & Testing Issued Wk 8 · Due Wk 10 Comparative aerofoil and drag-coefficient report; lift / drag polars across three aerofoils plus Reynolds-number commentary.
A3 Stability & Performance Issued Wk 11 · Due Wk 12 Stability case study with V-n diagram for a chosen aircraft and a written analysis of an FBW envelope-protection scheme.
