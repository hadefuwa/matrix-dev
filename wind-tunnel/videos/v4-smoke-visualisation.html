How Smoke Visualisation Works

Generating clean streamlines with the smoke generator, positioning the smoke wand for the cleanest capture, and using a phone camera to record slow-motion footage of the flow.

Key takeaways

01 · Warm-up

Smoke generator needs a short warm-up before it produces a steady visible plume. Start it before you turn the fan on.

02 · Low fan speed first

Use the lowest fan setting that still pulls smoke through. Higher speeds break up the streamlines into noise.

03 · Wand position

Hold the smoke outlet near the centre of the inlet grille. Move laterally to put the streamline onto the part of the body you want to study.

04 · Slow-motion video

Phone slow-mo (120–240 fps) captures eddies, separation and reattachment that the eye can't catch in real time.

Where this fits

Use this video in Week 4 (Airfoil Geometry & Boundary Layers) alongside Worksheet 6 — Streamlined?. The video gets learners producing clean streamlines fast; the worksheet then walks them through identifying and annotating flow features.
