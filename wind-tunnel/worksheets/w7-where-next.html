Worksheet 7 Week 10 · Secondary Controls & High-Lift Devices Learning Aim C

Where Next? — Independent Investigation

Choose your own engineering question, design a test article (3D-print it if you can), pick the right experimental procedure and run a complete investigation start to finish.

Overview01

Your turn

The previous six worksheets handed you a fixed procedure and a fixed body. This one is open-ended. You decide:

  • The engineering area you care about
  • The body you'll test (designed and ideally 3D-printed)
  • The procedure (pressure, force or smoke visualisation) you'll follow
  • The variable(s) you'll sweep and the data you'll capture

Learning objectives

  • Plan, justify and execute an independent wind-tunnel investigation
  • Select the correct procedure for the question being asked
  • Design a test article that fits the tunnel and produces meaningful data
  • Write up findings in a report format suitable for BTEC L3 Assignment 2
Pick your area02

Areas of interest

Pick the one that most interests you — or propose your own to your tutor:

  • Car body shape — fastback vs notchback rear; effect of a small rear spoiler
  • Aeronautics — different aerofoil profiles, leading-edge slats, winglets, blended wing-body
  • Ship hull design — half-submerged geometries (limited by the tunnel's air-only flow but you can still study near-surface bluff body drag)
  • Wind turbine efficiency — blade pitch, blade chord-length, blade twist
  • High-lift devices (Wk 10 focus) — flap deflection on a NACA aerofoil, effect of a slot or slat
  • Sports aerodynamics — cycling helmet shapes, dimpled vs smooth balls (extend WS5), F1 wing details
Recommended for Week 10 Studying a flap on an aerofoil ties directly into the BTEC Wk 10 content on secondary controls. Print a NACA 0015 with a slotted flap at 0°, 10°, 20°, 30° and compare lift/drag.
Design rules03

Test article guidelines

  • The wind tunnel test area is 125 mm × 125 mm.
  • Your shape's frontal area must be less than 10% of the cross-section (so < 1,560 mm² frontal). Bigger and you'll get severe blockage corrections.
  • Mount via a threaded insert compatible with the two-force module shaft.
  • If you want pressure measurements, include a hollow tapping that you can connect with tubing to the controller's pressure port (see WS2 / WS3 method).
  • Keep wall thickness ≥ 1.5 mm and add a small fillet at the mount — these are the parts most likely to fail.
Workflow Sketch → CAD model (Fusion 360 / OnShape) → check frontal area < 10% → 3D-print at 30% infill → tap-test for fit → run a low-speed sanity check before full sweep.
Pick a procedure04

Match your question to a method

If your question is about pressure:

Use the method from WS2 (cylinder) or WS3 (aerofoil). Include surface tappings in your CAD model, connect them to the controller's pressure port one at a time, and compute Cp.

If your question is about forces:

Use the method from WS4 (lift / drag) or WS5 (drag coefficient). Mount on the two-force module; sweep angle of attack and/or fan speed; record force readings.

If your question is about flow patterns:

Use the method from WS6 (smoke visualisation). Mount the object, use the smoke generator and capture stills + slow-motion video.

Plan05

Plan template

Before you switch anything on, complete this plan and have your tutor sign it off.

  1. Question — written as a single sentence (e.g. "Does a 10 mm rear spoiler reduce the drag coefficient of a notchback car body model?")
  2. Hypothesis — your predicted answer with brief reasoning
  3. Variables — independent (what you change), dependent (what you measure), controlled (what you keep constant)
  4. Test article — sketch + dimensions + frontal area check
  5. Procedure — which worksheet you're following, with any modifications
  6. Data plan — what readings, how many, in what range
  7. Risk assessment — moving parts, electrical, smoke, hot surfaces
  8. Expected analysis — graphs you'll plot, calculations you'll perform
Worked example06

Example brief — flap study

To anchor the format, here is a worked plan you could clone for a Wk 10 high-lift investigation:

QuestionHow does a plain flap deflected 0–30° change the lift coefficient of a NACA 0015 aerofoil at α = 5°?
HypothesisLift will increase with flap deflection up to ~25°, after which separation reduces gains. Drag will rise across the range.
VariablesIV: flap deflection (0°, 10°, 20°, 30°). DV: lift & drag forces. CV: aerofoil α = 5°, fan = 50%.
Test article3D-printed NACA 0015 with hinged flap, 25% chord. Frontal area < 10%.
ProcedureAdapted from WS4. Print four flap configurations rather than hinge in software.
Data planThree repeats at each flap angle. Plot CL and CD vs flap deflection. Compute L/D.
Report07

Report structure

Your final write-up should follow this structure (target ~1,500 words plus figures):

  1. Introduction — context, motivation, the question
  2. Theory — relevant equations and concepts from the unit so far
  3. Method — equipment, procedure, test article description, controlled variables
  4. Results — data tables, graphs and any annotated images
  5. Discussion — what the data shows; comparison to expectations / published values; sources of uncertainty
  6. Conclusion — answer to the question with confidence statement
  7. Further work — what you'd try next given more time
Wrap up08

What you should be able to do now

  • Plan and execute an independent wind-tunnel investigation
  • Choose the right procedure for an engineering question
  • Design and fabricate a tunnel-compatible test article
  • Write a clear, structured engineering report based on real data
